**Headline.** Retrieval, KB matching, citation grounding and privacy meet their targets. Overall
classification does not, for one identified reason: the dataset uses a 6 × 5 taxonomy (30
subcategories, incl. *Access & Security*), while the engine's label space is still the frozen
5 × 18 taxonomy in `shared/constants.py`, so 19 of the 30 subcategories cannot be predicted at
all. Where the gold label is predictable the engine is 97.5% accurate, and a taxonomy-agnostic kNN
reaches 95.5%. Fixing this is a `contract-change` to `shared/` (§7), not a model problem.

## 3. Method

- **Split** (§14.1): the generator split is used because it keeps every duplicate group inside one
  split (`ingest/split.py` verifies this, with a seeded stratified fallback). `make data-eval`
  builds `tickets_hist_eval` from the 1,055 train tickets only, so test tickets are never their own
  neighbours. KEDB and curated incidents are shared.
- **Systems.** *Engine* = P2's `ai.engine.run` end to end (signals → fused classifier → split →
  hybrid ticket search → KB ranking → priority → template synthesis → citation validation),
  offline, with P1's real stores and redaction, role `admin`. *kNN baseline* = P1's
  similarity-weighted kNN over the train split (the "kNN-only" ablation).
- **Sets.** Test split (220), demos (7) and test set (25); `--split val` (225) for tuning.
- **Relevance.** Similar tickets: same subcategory. KB: `gold_kb.json` (team labels for demos and
  test set; generator scenario link for sampled split tickets).

### 4.4 Similar and duplicate tickets (FR-02)

Test split, 220 queries against the train-only index:

| Retriever | Precision@5 | Success@5 | MRR@10 | nDCG@10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense (bge-small) | **0.947** | 0.991 | **0.992** | **0.884** |
| BM25 | 0.875 | 0.982 | 0.968 | 0.776 |
| Hybrid (RRF k = 60) | 0.934 | 0.991 | 0.989 | 0.872 |

BM25 keyword-trap rate (top-5 hits from another category): 7.7%, which is why BM25-only hits are
shown separately (§7.5).

Duplicates, full index (90 generator duplicates, query excluded): **exact-original recall@5 0.467**
(MRR@5 0.276), but **same-scenario recall@5 1.00** (precision@5 0.971). The generator writes
duplicates as paraphrases of a scenario that ~10 other tickets share, so the system reliably finds
the incident pattern but often ranks a sibling above the specific original. Real duplicates
(re-submissions) share far more text and land in the `DUPLICATE`/`LIKELY DUPLICATE` tiers.

## 5. Ablations (§14.3)

**Classifier.** kNN-only (P1 baseline) subcategory macro-F1 0.955 vs fused engine 0.223 (0.975
accuracy where it can emit the label). LLM-only and the full LLM fusion need a Groq key (`pytest -m
groq` / re-run `make eval` with `GROQ_API_KEY` set).

**Similar-ticket retrieval.** Dense alone is best on this corpus (§4.4). BM25 adds recall for exact
error codes but lowers precision@5 (0.947 → 0.934 hybrid); keep it, but keep BM25-only hits in the
separate keyword list.

**KB ranking** (P1 reference ranker, same weights as §7.6, n = 229):

| Variant | Hit@1 | Hit@3 |
|---|---|---|
| Full (semantic + BM25 + error codes + taxonomy) | 0.847 | 0.965 |
| Without BM25 | 0.843 | 0.969 |
| Without error-code index | 0.808 | 0.961 |
| Without taxonomy boost | 0.773 | 0.900 |
| Dense only | 0.738 | 0.921 |

The taxonomy boost (+7.4 pts Hit@1) and the error-code index (+3.9 pts Hit@1) carry the most
weight; BM25 adds little on top of them here.

**Verified incidents.** With them, 6/254 recommendations cite a Historical Incident; without
them, 0 can (synthetic history is never citable).

## 6. Observed errors (`eval/out/errors.jsonl`, 157 rows)

- 138 classification-only, 15 classification + KB, 4 KB-only.
- Most frequent confusions: MFA, Software Request, Hardware Request, Account Lockout, Password
  Reset → `Inquiry/Help/Other`; PostgreSQL and MySQL → MS SQL Server; Firewall → VPN;
  Laptop → CPU; Phishing/Malware → Software/Email; Keyboard/Mouse → Keyboard. All are labels the
  engine cannot emit.
- KB misses (19 on the split) cluster on generic request tickets (Hardware Request → KB-0016,
  Software Request → KB-0014) and on DB tickets where the engine-specific article competes with a
  generic one (e.g. INC0597 MySQL replica → KB-0001 instead of KB-0040).

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Contract change (ALL, `shared/`)**: replace `TAXONOMY` in `shared/constants.py` with the 6 × 5
   taxonomy in `config/taxonomy_map.yaml`, and add *Access & Security* to `BASELINE_PRIORITY` and
   `ASSIGNMENT_GROUP` (`IAM`). Expected effect: engine subcategory accuracy moves towards the 0.975
   it already achieves on emittable labels.
2. **P2 `config/ai.yaml`**: on the validation split the best kNN voter is `k = 10, power = 4`
   (macro-F1 0.982 vs 0.943 for `k = 15, power = 1`, `eval/out/tuning.json`). Keep `medium = 0.55`
   (≥ 75% accurate). Re-run `make eval-tune` for `high` after (1).
3. **P2**: contradiction detection and "1) … 2)" issue splitting (§4.7).
4. **P2 test**: `tests/p2_ai/test_kb_search.py` still expects the old `Hardware/Disk` mapping.
5. **P3**: once `backend/decision.py` lands, extend `eval/run.py` with decision accuracy and
   escalation recall against `expected_decision` (gold already in `data/test/test_tickets.json`).

## 8. Limitations

- The whole corpus, KEDB extension, curated incidents and test/trial tickets are synthetic or
  team-written; ticket texts are templated and more uniform than real ones, so retrieval metrics
  are optimistic.
- Split-ticket KB gold comes from the generator's scenario link, not from human labelling.
- Priority labels are a generator function of impact × urgency; agreement with them is a sanity
  check, not accuracy.
- All numbers are offline (no LLM). LLM classification/synthesis may change classification and
  step wording; citations and safety are enforced either way.
- Decision accuracy, escalation recall, live contract validity, the analyst usefulness rubric and
  the MTTR trial are not measured yet (they depend on P3's API and on the trial).
- The 60 curated incidents still need the human review in `data/seed/README.md`.

## 9. Synthetic-data statement

All historical tickets, their resolutions, timestamps, assignment groups, applications,
environments, locations and priorities are **synthetic**, produced by the seeded dataset generator
(`data/raw/dataset_generator.zip`). Every record lists these in `derived_fields`, and its
`resolution_origin` is `synthetic_kb_derived` or `synthetic_escalated` (raw value
`synthetic_generated` kept in `metadata`); such resolutions are shown to analysts with a SYNTHETIC
badge and are **never cited** as evidence. KB-0031…KB-0060 are team-authored. Curated incidents
(`curated_verified`) and test/trial tickets (`metadata.source = "synthetic_team"`) are
team-written with AI assistance. The MTTR historical baseline is synthetic and labelled as such.
